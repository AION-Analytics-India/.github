## Why would an individual use Sargas?

A phone may contain private conversations, accounts and work credentials. Sargas' reported development scope helps the owner inspect Android accessibility-service and device-administrator registrations, review locally refused domain lookups while an optional DNS filter is enabled, run available known-indicator diagnostic checks, and choose corrective action through Android settings.

The filter does not identify which app requested a domain. A full per-app contacts/SMS/camera/microphone/location permission inventory is **not complete**. No negative scan establishes an absence of spyware, and no real-world spyware detection accuracy has been published.

## Why would an institution evaluate Sargas?

Organisations may need to assess Android device risk before relying on a device for work processes. The owner-versus-institution data boundary matters: personal messages, contact lists, photos and browsing must not automatically become organisation-visible. The publicly described institutional location-integrity dashboard contains **synthetic** observations; actual workforce collection or central management is not validated. A controlled pilot should establish consent/lawful basis, data minimisation and clear human review.

## Four reported layers

1. Accessibility and device-administrator registration review.
2. Optional domain-filtering of listed DNS destinations, with local refusal history.
3. User-initiated known-indicator diagnostics and untestable-check explanations.
4. Owner-directed Android settings and remediation guidance.

VPN/Private DNS compatibility and unobserved traffic limit the filter. Google Play Protect remains complementary, not replaced.
