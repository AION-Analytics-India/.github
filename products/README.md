## Why an individual?

An unfamiliar accessibility service, device administrator, suspicious domain, or diagnostic finding can affect personal data and accounts. The current AION-described Android workflows help the phone owner inspect **some** privileged registrations, inspect **blocked** DNS domain requests when the optional filter is on, and review available known indicators. The user selects any correction in Android. This is **not** a complete installed-app sensitive-permission inventory and does **not** prove that an application used a permission.

## Why an institution?

When work credentials or sensitive business material are accessed from Android phones, organisations need a bounded, explainable way to evaluate risk without assuming a right to collect employees' contacts, messages, photos or personal browsing. AION's published location-check-in dashboard uses **synthetic** data; no functioning mobile-device fleet console or centralised enterprise telemetry has been verified. An institutional pilot should verify scope, consent/lawful basis, assessment evidence, support burden and reporting minimisation.

## Four documented layers

1. **Powerful access review** — accessibility services and device administrators reported by Android.
2. **Optional domain filter** — can refuse listed DNS domains while active and retain local refusals; no per-app requester attribution.
3. **User-initiated diagnostics** — known indicator checks on available data; negative result is not a clean bill of health.
4. **Guided remediation** — explanation and Android settings; no automatic prevention of root compromise or guarantee of malware removal.

The DNS filter relies on Android VPN service and may conflict with another VPN or specified Private DNS, and cannot observe all direct-IP or unsupported network traffic. Keep Google Play Protect enabled.
